UniFi Protect (API) talks to your console over its official Integration API using an API key — no username or password is stored.

Connection
Console
The IP address or hostname of your UniFi console. Restart required.
API Key
Created in UniFi Site Manager under Integrations. It is sent only after the console's certificate matches the one this install trusts, and never appears in the log. Restart required.
Test Connection
Checks the certificate, then the key, then lists your devices. The first successful test pins the certificate; if it ever changes, the connection is refused before the key is sent and you are asked to confirm the new one.
Defaults
Maximum concurrent live views
A cap for the whole host, not per camera. Blank picks from the encoder found — 6 with hardware, 2 with software. A request past the cap is refused with a logged reason instead of overloading the machine. Restart required.
ffmpeg path
Blank searches /usr/bin, /usr/local/bin, then PATH. Restart required.
iCloud+ plan
Drives the recording warning only. Apple caps HomeKit Secure Video by camera count: 1 on 50 GB, 5 on 200 GB, unlimited on 2 TB and above. Footage does not count against your iCloud storage quota.
Per-camera defaults
What every device inherits. Changing one moves every camera that has not overridden it on the Devices tab; a camera showing overridden keeps its own value until you press reset.
Devices
Expose in HomeKit
Whether this device is bridged at all. Takes effect on the next discovery.
Live view quality
Auto follows the resolution HomeKit asks for: 640×360 and below uses the low substream, 1280×720 medium, larger high. Pinning a substream overrides that.
Live view audio
HomeKit is told which audio codecs a camera offers once, when the accessory is set up. Turning audio on therefore only reaches HomeKit after a restart; turning it off applies to the next live view. Restart required.
Two-way audio
Talk back through a camera with a speaker. Fixed at accessory setup for the same reason as audio. Restart required.
HomeKit Secure Video
Off by default and enabled per camera. A recording camera runs one continuous ffmpeg whether or not anything moves — that is what lets a clip start before the motion — costing roughly 15% of one core each on hardware encoding. Restart required.
Package camera
Offered only for cameras whose payload reports the downward lens. It appears as a second accessory at 2 fps, does not record, and therefore does not use one of your iCloud plan's camera slots. Takes effect on the next discovery.
Hardware transcoding

Every Protect camera streams HEVC and HomeKit accepts only H.264, so every live view is a transcode. At startup the plugin probes ffmpeg, prefers Intel Quick Sync then VAAPI, and trial-encodes each one rather than trusting -encoders to list it; software is the last resort. The encoder it settled on is logged at startup.

The difference is not marginal: measured on an i7-8700K with UHD 630, 20 seconds of 2688×1512 costs 1.79 s of CPU via VAAPI and 49.1 s via libx264 — about 27×. That is why the live-view cap defaults to 6 on hardware and 2 on software.

Seeing h264_vaapi rather than h264_qsv on an Intel host is normal, not a fault. A warning naming libx264 means hardware acceleration is not reaching the process — most often the render device is not passed into the Docker container (--device=/dev/dri), or the Homebridge user is not in the render group. Run Homebridge with DEBUG logging to see which candidates were rejected and why.

Recording and live views share the GPU

A recording camera does not consume one of the concurrent live-view slots — that cap exists to protect interactive viewing, and a recording process is not an interactive viewer. The two do however share the same encoder. Several cameras recording continuously reduce the headroom available to live views, so on a host near its limit, lower the live-view cap rather than assuming the two budgets are independent.
